## A useful launch sequence

1. Publish one short LinkedIn walkthrough with the live URL and the animated tour. Lead with the problem: understanding how banking records connect.
2. A few days later, share one technical lesson: why a balanced journal can still be wrong. Link to the exact negative-data test.
3. Share a domain-focused example with an appropriate community where project posts are welcome. Ask for review of one modeling decision, not a generic request for stars.
4. Respond to feedback. Turn reproducible problems into small issues and thank contributors in release notes.
5. Add a versioned GitHub release once you have completed your own walkthrough. Keep screenshots and claims current as the model evolves.

## Draft launch post

I built Banking Model Atlas because a banking schema is easier to understand when you can follow the business story.

You can explore 80 entities across 16 domains, inspect columns and relationships, and follow journeys from a disputed card purchase to ledger reconciliation. It includes a runnable DuckDB schema and synthetic examples with tests for reversals, lifecycle transitions and incorrect balances.

The model is fictional, and the site distinguishes tested examples from schema-only extensions. I’d welcome feedback on the model and explanations—especially where you would make a different design choice.
